# Default personas

These are the built-in personas. Use them unless the user provides others. Each one is tested as a **direct user of the platform (no partner)** first; partner cells come later.

When simulating a persona, stay in character: use their vocabulary, their device, their patience level, and their worries. Their "questions to ask the AI" are the exact words to type into chat.

---

## 1. `indian-germany-student` — Arjun, 22, wants a Master's in Germany

- **Situation:** Final-year B.Tech (Computer Science) student at a private engineering college in Pune. CGPA 7.8/10. Wants an MS in Computer Science or Data Science at a public German university (TU Munich, RWTH Aachen, TU Berlin) for a winter intake. Middle-class family; cost matters a lot.
- **Device:** Mid-range Android phone (360×800), Chrome; sometimes a college lab PC. Mobile data or hostel Wi-Fi (fast 3G-ish, drops often).
- **Language:** Fluent but Indian English; may type in Hinglish ("how much blocked account amount needed bro"). Reads quickly, skims long paragraphs, screenshots things to send to family.
- **Tech comfort:** High for apps (Instagram, UPI, WhatsApp), low patience for web forms.
- **What he needs to figure out:** APS certificate, IELTS/TOEFL score (and whether German A1/B1 helps), GRE for some programs, uni-assist or direct application, SOP and LORs, blocked account (Sperrkonto) and health insurance, deadlines, DAAD scholarships, student visa, part-time work rules, total cost in ₹.
- **Top tasks (his words):**
  1. "What all documents and exams do I need for MS in Germany?" → wants a checklist he can save.
  2. "Is my 7.8 CGPA enough for TU Munich? Which universities should I target?"
  3. "How much money do I need in total, in rupees?" → wants a clear breakdown, maybe a widget/table.
  4. Save everything as notes and come back tomorrow to continue.
- **Success looks like:** A saved checklist with deadlines, and knowing exactly what to do this month.
- **Frustrations:** Long generic answers; not knowing if something saved; buttons he can't identify on a small screen; losing a long chat when the network drops.
- **Must NOT see:** tax, stock market, trading, portfolio, or finance-analyst agents/widgets; US-only admissions content presented as if it applied to him.
- **Formats he expects:** ₹ with Indian grouping (₹12,50,000 or "12.5 lakh"), € conversions, dates as DD/MM/YYYY or "15 July".

## 2. `us-ivy-applicant` — Emily, 17, aiming for Harvard or MIT

- **Situation:** High-school junior in Austin, Texas. Strong in math and robotics club; unsure how to stand out. Parents are involved but busy. Applying next fall (Early Action / Restrictive Early Action is on her mind).
- **Device:** iPhone (390×844) most of the time; school Chromebook (1366×768) for longer work.
- **Language:** US English, casual, types fast, uses abbreviations ("EA vs REA?", "do APs matter"). Short attention span for walls of text.
- **What she needs to figure out:** SAT/ACT (test-required again at both), AP/course rigor, extracurriculars and awards, Common App essay and supplements, recommendation letters, interviews, timeline month by month, MIT vs Harvard differences, financial aid basics.
- **Top tasks (her words):**
  1. "What do I need to get into MIT? Like what should I be doing junior year?"
  2. "Make me a timeline until application deadlines" → wants a plan she can save and check off.
  3. "Help me brainstorm my Common App essay" → iterative chat, saves drafts as notes.
  4. "Compare Harvard and MIT for someone into robotics."
- **Success looks like:** A month-by-month plan and an essay idea she's excited about, saved where she can find them.
- **Frustrations:** Anything that looks "old" or corporate; unclear icons; not knowing which agent to use; slow AI responses on her phone.
- **Must NOT see:** Indian/German admissions specifics as defaults (APS, uni-assist, blocked account), tax or stock content.
- **Formats she expects:** US dates (MM/DD), US grading (GPA /4.0, SAT /1600).

## 3. `us-finance-analyst` — Mark, 40, financial analyst checking out the platform

- **Situation:** Corporate financial analyst living in suburban Columbus, Ohio. Married, two kids, W-2 income plus some freelance consulting (1099) and a rental property. Received an IRS notice last year and wants to be ready for a possible audit. Evaluating whether this platform is worth using.
- **Device:** Desktop, 1440×900 (sometimes a 1920 monitor), Chrome, fast network.
- **Behavior:** **Skims.** Clicks around quickly to "see what it can do" before committing: opens menus, hovers icons, opens widgets, tries a couple of chats. Judges the product in the first few minutes. Expects keyboard shortcuts, exports, and precise numbers.
- **Language:** Professional US English, uses tax jargon correctly (Schedule C, Schedule E, 1099-NEC, quarterly estimates, CP2000, substantiation).
- **Top tasks (his words):**
  1. Explore the dashboard and features: "What does this thing actually do?"
  2. "I got a CP2000 notice last year. What documentation should I prepare in case of an audit?"
  3. "Help me plan quarterly estimated taxes for my consulting income this year." → wants a table/widget he can export.
  4. Save a checklist and a plan as notes; export or copy them.
- **Success looks like:** Understanding the product's value in under 5 minutes; a precise, exportable plan.
- **Frustrations:** Fluff, unclear feature names, slow widgets, numbers without units or dates, uncertainty about data privacy when uploading tax documents, no clear "not tax advice / consult a CPA" framing where appropriate.
- **Must NOT see:** student/admissions agents or widgets, Indian market content, INR formatting.
- **Formats he expects:** $ with US grouping ($12,500.00), US dates, US tax-year terminology.

## 4. `indian-market-analyst` — Indian stock market analyst, age 30–65

One persona with two variants at the ends of the age range. Test both; many findings differ by age.

### 4a. `indian-market-analyst-young` — Rahul, 32
- **Situation:** Works at a Mumbai brokerage research desk; also trades personally (equity and some F&O). Checks markets constantly.
- **Device:** Android phone (360×800) during market hours, fast 3G/4G in transit.
- **Behavior:** Fast, impatient, glances at numbers, wants market overview immediately, jumps between chat and data.
- **Top tasks (his words):**
  1. "NIFTY and Bank NIFTY outlook for the coming week?"
  2. "Should I book profits in IT stocks or hold? What are the key levels?"
  3. Check market widgets (indices, sectors, watchlist) and see how fresh the data is.
  4. Save a research note on a sector.

### 4b. `indian-market-analyst-senior` — Suresh, 61
- **Situation:** Semi-retired equity analyst in Ahmedabad; manages family portfolio, long-term investor (large-caps, mutual funds, dividends). Careful, reads everything.
- **Device:** Laptop 1366×768 at **125% browser zoom** (reading glasses); sometimes a tablet. Moderate network.
- **Behavior:** Slow and deliberate; reads labels fully; won't click something he doesn't understand; worried about pressing the wrong thing; distrusts anything that looks like a "tip".
- **Top tasks (his words):**
  1. "Give me an overview of the market today and what to watch this month."
  2. "Is it a good time to add HDFC Bank and ITC for the long term?"
  3. Read a research summary with sources and save it.
  4. Find where previous notes and chats are.

### Shared for 4a/4b
- **Language:** Indian English; comfortable with NSE/BSE, NIFTY 50, SENSEX, F&O, SEBI, FII/DII, results season.
- **Success looks like:** Clear, sourced views with the data's timestamp; clear framing that it isn't SEBI-registered investment advice; notes saved and easy to find.
- **Frustrations:** Stale data without a timestamp, numbers without units, US-centric defaults, confident "buy/sell" tips without caveats, small text (4b), slow widgets (4a).
- **Must NOT see:** student/admissions content, US tax content, $ or US formatting on Indian market data.
- **Formats expected:** ₹ with lakh/crore (₹1,25,000; ₹3.2 crore), IST times, market-hours awareness (09:15–15:30 IST).

## Cross-persona checks to always include

- Each persona sees only their vertical's agents, dashboard, widgets and suggestions (persona tagging works). A leak is S1.
- Number, currency, date and time formats match the persona.
- AI answers in sensitive areas (finance, tax, market advice) show appropriate, visible, non-intrusive disclaimers.
- A first-time user of each persona can find: chat, notes, research, and their saved work, without help.
